For decades, we've organized digital information the same way we organize physical objects: by putting them in containers. Files go in folders. Emails go in inboxes. Notes go in notebooks. This metaphor served us well when storage was expensive and retrieval was manual.

But containers are fundamentally limiting. They force hierarchical thinking onto information that is inherently networked. A note about a conversation with a client belongs in the client folder, the project folder, and the meeting notes folder simultaneously. We've been forcing square pegs into round holes.

## The Spatial Computing Shift

The rise of AI changes everything. When machines can understand content semantically, the container becomes obsolete. Why file a document when the system can surface it based on meaning, context, and relevance?

This isn't just about better search. It's a fundamental shift in how we relate to information. Instead of asking "where did I put that?", we ask "what do I need right now?" The system becomes an extension of thought rather than a filing cabinet.

## What Comes Next

We're building tools that embrace this shift. Unbody treats all your content—documents, images, videos, conversations—as a unified knowledge space. No folders. No manual organization. Just intelligent retrieval based on what matters.

The container era served us well. But it's time to move on. The future of information is fluid, contextual, and deeply personal. We're excited to be building it.
