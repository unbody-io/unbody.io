Pregnancy loss is one of the most common griefs there is, and one of the least supported by software — the medical system tends to close the file once someone is physically cleared, and the emotional part is left to whoever happens to be nearby. We partnered with the team behind Sibyl to build a private companion that understands where someone actually is in a loss, and stays with them as that changes.

Grief isn't one timeline, so Sibyl tracks the emotional, physical, and practical sides of a loss as three arcs that move independently — and converge asymmetrically, so distress is met within days while one hopeful message doesn't graduate someone out of the hard part. Memory is derived from the person's own data at read time rather than extracted into a profile; a safety layer is tuned to err toward caution in telling past harm from present intent; and what Sibyl surfaces is chosen by a deterministic, auditable process — the AI writes the words, not the decisions.

The point was never to make a system that claims to understand grief, but one where the *person* feels known — software that learns the human and adapts to them, with craft as a form of care. Sibyl is in private beta through a waitlist, and has been recognized through Springboard Enterprises' women's health accelerator and FemTech Insider's coverage of pregnancy-loss care.
